# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Primary visitors are international educators, cultural partners, institutions, and families encountering Universo Nahiara through public-facing event pages, campaigns, and editorial surfaces. They arrive from shared links, partner referrals, and search, often with little prior context and a short attention window. On this event page in particular, they need instant clarity that the event is real, public, educational, and worth forwarding.

## Product Purpose
Association Nahiara presents a cultural and educational ecosystem around flowers, pollinators, biodiversity, and global citizenship. The website exists to make that ecosystem legible, credible, and memorable across institutional, public, and international contexts. Success means a visitor can quickly understand the initiative, trust its seriousness, and share or act on a page without needing additional explanation.

## Brand Personality
International, botanical, ceremonial.

The brand should feel cultivated rather than corporate, public-facing rather than commercial, and emotionally uplifting without slipping into soft NGO vagueness. It should communicate seriousness, educational value, and cultural dignity while remaining welcoming to families and children.

## Anti-references
Do not make this look like a generic NGO brochure, a municipal event flyer dropped into a webpage, or a safe editorial template with interchangeable serif-led sections and beige restraint.

Avoid:
- Bland conference-event layouts with stacked info cards and weak visual hierarchy
- Generic “green sustainability” pages that lean on stock eco tropes instead of a distinct identity
- Overly academic or museum-dry pages that lose the warmth and invitation of the event

## Design Principles
- Make the page shareable in one glance: date, place, audience, and purpose must read immediately.
- Treat biodiversity as culture, not just information: the visual language should feel ceremonial, alive, and crafted.
- Build trust through structure: institutional clarity, canonical metadata, bilingual readability, and strong event framing are part of the design.
- Preserve wonder without childishness: the page should welcome families while still feeling appropriate for European education and institutional partners.
- Use distinction over decoration: every visual move should strengthen recall and credibility, not add generic “design.”

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard accessibility. Support bilingual reading without forcing route changes. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences. Mobile readability is non-negotiable because the page will be opened from shared links and messaging apps. Content should remain understandable for first-time visitors who do not know the project beforehand.
